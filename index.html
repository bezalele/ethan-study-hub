THE COURSE STORY

Start here. Then zoom in.

Each chapter answers one big question. Open a chapter only when you are ready for the details.

01
FOUNDATIONS · 15–22%

Why was American government designed this way?

From independence → a weak first government → Constitution → federalism.

+

See the story

Colonies → Declaration → Articles → Convention → Constitution → Bill of Rights.

Then zoom into

Democratic ideals · types of democracy · government power & rights · Articles of Confederation · ratification · principles of government · federalism.

Anchor evidence

Foundational documents and the constitutional arguments that explain why the system was built this way.

02
BRANCHES · 25–36%

Who has power — and who can stop them?

Congress, president, courts, and bureaucracy share power and compete over policy.

+

See the system

Congress makes laws. The president executes them. Courts interpret them. The bureaucracy carries policy out.

Then zoom into

House & Senate · congressional powers & behavior · presidency · presidential power · courts · judicial review · bureaucracy · policymaking.

Connect everything

Checks and balances are the thread: every institution has power, but none acts completely alone.

03
LIBERTIES & RIGHTS · 13–18%

What can government do — and what must it not do?

The Constitution protects liberty while government also tries to maintain order and equality.

+

See the distinction

Civil liberties protect people from government. Civil rights concern equal treatment and protection.

Then zoom into

Bill of Rights · religion · speech · press · Second Amendment · due process · privacy · incorporation · equal protection · social movements · affirmative action.

Cases matter here

Supreme Court decisions show how broad constitutional language becomes rules applied to real disputes.

04
BELIEFS & IDEOLOGY · 10–15%

Why do Americans disagree about what government should do?

Beliefs are shaped by experience, political socialization, events, ideology, and public opinion.

+

See the human layer

Institutions do not operate in a vacuum. Citizens bring different values and expectations to politics.

Then zoom into

Political attitudes · socialization · ideology · political events · polling · evaluating data · party ideology · economic and social policy.

Practice the skill

This unit is a natural place to learn how to read polls, graphs, samples, trends, and claims carefully.

05
PARTICIPATION · 20–27%

How do people influence government?

Voting is only one path. Parties, interest groups, campaigns, elections, and media connect people to power.

+

See the loop

People form opinions → organize → vote and campaign → choose leaders → influence policy → react to results.

Then zoom into

Voting rights & behavior · turnout · parties · third parties · interest groups · presidential & congressional elections · campaigns · finance · media.

Bring it together

This chapter connects citizens back to every institution studied earlier in the course.

FOUNDING STORY

How did a country get built from disagreement?

One tension drives the founding: How do you make government strong enough to work, but limited enough to protect liberty?

🧠 Ethan's mental model

Britain felt too powerful → Articles were too weak → Constitution seeks balance.
